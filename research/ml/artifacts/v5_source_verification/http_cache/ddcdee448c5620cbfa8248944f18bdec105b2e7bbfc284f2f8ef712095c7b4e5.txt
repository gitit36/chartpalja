HISTORY DETAILS CREDITS SYNOPSIS GENRE Print Details Full page view HISTORY The 8 Nov 1968 DV noted that Fanfare Film Productions, Inc., was making preparations for the upcoming project. Principal photography began 2 Dec 1968, according to a 6 Dec 1968 DV production chart. On 27 Jan 1969, DV reported that the $260,000 film was in post-production. It marked the directorial debut of actor Jack Starrett . Editing, sound mixing and sound effects dubbing duties were contracted to Synchrofilm , Producers Sound Service , and Edit-Rite , as reported in the 2 Dec 1968, 11 Dec 1968, and 25 Feb 1969 issues of DV. The 26 Mar 1969 Var announced the film’s 18 Apr 1969 New York City debut. An article in the 23 Apr 1969 Var stated that a nationwide release was scheduled for 28 May 1969 at 275 venues, with another 625 planned over the ensuing three months. A Los Angeles, CA, opening followed on 4 Jun 1969. Despite mixed reviews, Run, Angel, Run! was listed in Var among the top-grossing releases between 7 May and 23 Jul 1969. The 11 Mar 1970 Var mentioned the picture as an entry to that year’s Valladolid Film Festival in Spain. A Los Angeles, CA, opening followed on 4 Jun 1969. Despite mixed reviews, Run, Angel, Run! was listed in Var among the top-grossing releases between 7 May and 23 Jul 1969. The 11 Mar 1970 Var mentioned the picture as an entry to that year’s Valladolid Film Festival in Spain. An article in the 14 May 1969 Var noted that Jack Starrett was entitled to five percent of Fanfare’s share of gross receipts. Nearly two years later, the 21 Apr 1971 issue revealed that Starrett was suing Fanfare for $11,800 in unpaid wages. Within weeks, the 12 May 1971 edition reported a countersuit by Fanfare president Joe Solomon against Starrett for $6,626.22 in “loans and money allegedly advanced him.” Run, Angel, Run! continued in theaters for at least another year, as indicated by a 31 May 1972 Var box-office report. The film’s “R” rating was later changed to “PG” by the Motion Picture Association of America (MPAA), as stated in the 19 Feb 1975 Var. Several sources note that screenwriter V. A. Furlong was actress Valerie Starrett . With his former buddies in murderous pursuit, Angel outwits the gang by driving through a railroad freight yard and finding refuge at a northern California sheep ranch. Dan Felton, the owner of the ranch and a former biker himself, gives Angel a job and rents him a shack, which Lauri converts into a comfortable home. As four of the Advocates continue to search for him, Angel gradually comes to accept his new, peaceful life with Lauri. Eventually, Dan's teenaged daughter, Meg, meets the four Advocates at a roadhouse and tells them about the young man with a beautiful motorcycle living on her father's ranch. Now certain of Angel's whereabouts, the gang brutally gang-rapes Meg and then rides to the shack and attacks Lauri. When Angel appears, a fierce battle ensues between the four gang members on bikes and Angel on foot.
